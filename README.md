# SUIO: Suicide Ontology

The Suicide Ontology (SUIO) is a biomedical ontology in the domain of sucide.

Suicide is a leading cause of death during adolescence and adulthood. In the USA, over 1.5 million people attempt suicide each year, with ~50,000 dying by suicide. Suicide is associated with complex and multifactorial psychological, cognitive, and behavioral processes, but is considered a preventable cause of death. Despite extensive research exploring relations among suicide and psychological, neurological, and environmental factors, the associations among these factors remains unclear. Many genes have been found to be associated with high suicide susceptibility; however, the role of genes represents a particularly important, yet under-explored, domain of research.  

SUIO was previously named as the Ontology of Human Suicide (OHS), which was initiated in 2015 when Dr. Xiaofu Pan and Dr. Shunzhou Deng were visiting scholars in the University of Michigan and working with Dr. Oliver He there. The early OHS work was focused on annotation and represention of suicide genes. The work was idled for many years. We have resume the suicide ontology development since 2025, and officially changed the name to SUIO: Suicide Ontology on April 28, 2026. 

SUIO is now developed to represent suicide-related information including suicide, suicide types, genes, proteins, diseases, and the relations among these entities. SUIO ontologically defines many other suicide-related process terms such as suicide ideation, prevention, treatment, and management. SUIO models and represents environmental and individual risk factors (e.g., alcohol, stress, solitude, and family inheritance) contributing to suicide. Furthermore, SUIO is being used as the platform to develop an integrative ontological design pattern of suicide behavior as informed by leading psychological theories of suicide, including the Interpersonal Theory of Suicide (Van Orden et al., 2010. Ref: PMID: [20438238](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20438238/).   

Now the SUIO research has become part of the NIH-funded PHASES project:  https://github.com/Buffalo-Ontology-Group/phases.
